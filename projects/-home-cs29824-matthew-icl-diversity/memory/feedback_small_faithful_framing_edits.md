When the user gives framing feedback on prose (paper, doc, planning artifact), default to the **smallest faithful directional edit** — nudge the existing prose toward what they asked for; do NOT add new conceptual scaffolding ("inseparable," "second-tier," "two halves matter," extra clarifying machinery). If an edit feels too small to be interesting to write, it's probably right.

**Why:** in conversation 2026-04-27 on `paper/human-ai-creativity-workshop.md` the user pushed back five times on the Tevet framing. Each pushback triggered me to add new scaffolding ("inseparable" → "two halves are separable but here's DecTest as counter-proof" → ...) until the user said "stop overstating things." The lean version that finally landed at round seven was what they wanted at round two. The four intervening rounds were oscillation between extremes driven by my urge to elaborate.

**How to apply:**
- Treat framing comments as direction signals, not as full reframings. "Community is small" doesn't mean "make it a footnote" — it means "shift the centre of gravity."
- Watch for the urge to add new conceptual structure: numbered tiers, "inseparable" / "two halves" claims, explicit second-tier callouts, generalizing language. If I'm reaching for that, stop and just edit the existing sentence to point closer to the user's direction.
- **Exception — bigger edits ARE warranted when the user signals it.** Examples that explicitly authorise an overhaul: "$E$ is boring and uninteresting in its uninterestingness," "the previous author had no idea what the paper was about," "do a real overhaul," "fresh eyes." Big language → big edit. Small directional comment → small edit. The signal is in the user's language, not in my interpretation of how much the framing needs to shift.
- When the size of the edit isn't obvious from the user's language, ask one focused question ("do you want Tevet co-equal to OLMo or secondary?") instead of guessing and burning round-trips.
